Todd Harple and Olympic AI

Olympic sport provides a useful real-world environment for studying the human interface with AI because technology must operate across athletes, federations, organizers, broadcasters, venues, partners, and governing institutions.

Documented pathway

  1. Intel Olympics program. Harple worked on Olympic innovation and AI at Intel across the company's Olympic sponsorship era.
  2. AI in practice. His work included pathfinding and roadmapping around applications such as computer vision, 3D athlete tracking, venue intelligence, digital twins and athlete-facing AI.
  3. IOC collaboration. Harple participated in the IOC AI Working Group during development of the Olympic AI Agenda.
  4. Olympic AI Agenda. The IOC launched the Olympic AI Agenda in April 2024 as a holistic strategy for AI and sport. The IOC's announcement includes an AI Working Group item among the launch materials.

Why this matters to the human-interface thesis

The Olympic environment makes the organizational dimension of AI unusually visible. A technically successful system still has to fit relationships among international federations, national Olympic committees, athletes, organizing committees, broadcasters, technology partners and the IOC. In a 2024 interview, Harple described the Olympic ecosystem as complex and discussed the need to understand how its participants interact before deploying technology.
